The Shape of an Evening

Ninety minutes of teaching.
Ninety minutes of work.

Every session runs to the same shape. The break sits inside the second half, because the room getting to know each other is a goal of this programme rather than an interruption to it. And nobody leaves an evening without having said a number out loud and had it written down — that is the mechanism the whole programme is built around.

The Taiwan Spine

Five things that run through
every single session.

Almost every venture curriculum in the world quietly assumes Delaware — US SAFEs, NVCA model docs, US endowment LPs, a NASDAQ exit. For an investor operating in Taipei, the questions that actually decide outcomes are somewhere else entirely. These five run underneath the whole programme.

01
The listing ladder
Emerging Board (興櫃) → TPEx → TWSE, plus the Innovation Board. A great many Taiwanese companies list at a size that could not go public in the US at all. That changes what a good outcome looks like here — and therefore what you should be willing to pay going in.
02
The FA layer
A broker layer sitting between founders and capital, with no real Silicon Valley equivalent. If you don't know who is advising a company, you are reading half the deal.
03
Corporate money
Corporate investors took part in roughly 72% of Taiwan deals by deal count, against about 22% for traditional VCs. The person across the table is more often a corporate than a fund — and they want something structurally different from the same company.
04
The Series A gap
Angel money exists and pre-IPO money exists. The middle thins out exactly where a company needs capital to go international — which is where a well-positioned investor here has the most room.
05
Structures that don't appear in textbooks
Redeemable convertible preferred shares, performance conditions, buyback terms, and the ownership levels that were normal in this region a decade ago and have not entirely gone. Read the paper and you understand the behaviour of a market.

How It Works

Three mechanisms
carry the whole programme.

Most programmes teach venture capital and then hope a network forms in the chat group. This one is built the other way round — the room is the structure, and the teaching happens inside it.

01

Every visiting investor brings a live deal

Not a case study and not a tidy one-pager — a real deal from their own current pipeline, anonymised to whatever degree they need. The room runs it through whichever stage of the investment cycle that session covers.

02

The cohort writes back

After each session the cohort produces a short research memo for that investor, on a sector or company they care about, within a fortnight. It is the fee the room pays for access — and it is the best diligence practice in the programme.

03

The Deal Book runs from week one

Every deal you see, dated, with the position you took and why. By the end you hold roughly twenty dated positions you have defended out loud. That is a track record — and it is the asset that gets a person hired into venture.

What Happens in the Room

Six role-plays.
No observers.

Every workshop runs to the same grammar: a brief, a clock, a position, the reveal, and a log. The visiting investor plays whichever seat is hardest, and can stop the clock to narrate what they are thinking. Everyone is cast — nobody watches.

The Meeting

The visiting investor plays a founder from a real deal they took. You run the meeting in rotation while the rest of the room scores against a rubric — then they reveal what actually happened, and which question would have surfaced it.

The Kill List

A real deal from the investor's pipeline, and forty-five minutes to produce the five questions that would kill it — ranked by how fast each would resolve. They score your list against the diligence they actually ran, and name the question the room missed.

Ten Minutes to a Verdict

Three real forecast models, ten minutes each. Mark the three assumptions carrying the outcome and state a verdict. Then the investor walks their own markup — not a teardown, a demonstration of where a trained eye goes first.

The Negotiation

A three-way role-play, rotating: lead investor, founder, and a corporate co-investor with a strategic agenda. Your own marked-up term sheets are the working documents.

Both Sides of the Table

One real deal with a corporate term sheet on it. Teams argue the CVC's position, the financial co-investor's, and the founder's — then swap and argue the opposite. The corporate investors judge which arguments would actually have moved them.

The Investment Committee

The final rep. Your team presents a full IC memo on a deal the cohort sourced, to a panel of the investors who taught the programme. They interrupt, they ask the question nobody prepared for, and then they vote — and each of them says why.

Why this method.

Junior VCs don't fail because they lack frameworks — they fail because they've never seen a real term sheet, never built a diligence set under time pressure, and never had a working investor tell them why a deal was a pass. We've spent 14 years on the investor's side of the table, scouting for Volkswagen and Audi and evaluating hundreds of startups for CES and VivaTech. VC Academy is that experience, structured into reps you can actually practice.

Common Questions

Before you reserve.

Do I need finance or investing experience?

No. The program is built for people trying to break in — business students, aspiring analysts, operators, and founders. We start from how the ecosystem works and build up to the deal mechanics.

I missed the launch event — can I still apply?

Yes. The free launch event on August 14 was an introduction to the program, not a requirement. Applications for the founding cohort are open to everyone — you can read the recap of that afternoon to see what it covered.

When and where does it run?

In person, at the Mosaic Venture Lab office in Taipei. Sessions are scheduled around participants' working commitments — exact dates and times are confirmed with applicants once the room is set.

How big is the cohort?

Small by design — about 10 participants for the founding cohort, so everyone gets direct time with working investors.

What does it cost?

Wave 1: NT$30,000 for the full programme — 60% off the NT$75,000 list price. Later waves step up to NT$31,500 and NT$34,500. All 12 sessions across six months, in person; materials, live deals and time with visiting investors are included. A government subsidy application is in progress.

Is my seat confirmed when I pay?

Your seat is held the moment you pay. The organizer reserves the right to review eligibility — if we can't confirm your seat after review, you'll receive a full refund.

Can I pay in installments, or have my company pay?

Yes — 3 or 6 credit-card installments, same total either way. We can also issue a company invoice with a tax ID (統編).

What do I get after the programme?

The six things you built — thesis, Deal Book, diligence method, marked-up term sheet, cap table, IC memo — plus the VC Bible, an MVL Badge & Certificate and a place in the VC Alumni Group. Selected graduates are also considered for deal-flow sharing, a recommendation letter, an MVL internship and sitting in on MVL's deal reviews — subject to evaluation, and not guaranteed.
